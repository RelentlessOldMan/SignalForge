# ADR 0303: ADSR envelope

Status: accepted, 2026-11-17.

## Context

The attack-decay-sustain-release envelope shapes the loudness of a synthesized note. The level
rises to full while a key is pressed, falls to a sustain level and holds, then dies away when
the key is let go. Multiplying a waveform by it gives a note. It is a waveform generator, so
it lives beside `pulse_train`, in whole samples.

## Decision

- **The API.**
  - Python: `adsr(num_samples, attack, decay, sustain, release, gate)` in `waveforms`.
  - C#: `Waveforms.Adsr(numSamples, attack, decay, sustain, release, gate)`.
  - Every argument but `sustain` is an integer count of samples. `gate` is the sample where
    the key is let go.
- **The value.** Write `A`, `D`, `S`, `R` and `G`.
  - Held, `h(n)` for `n ≥ 0`:
    - `n/A` for `n < A`, rising from 0;
    - `1 − (1 − S)·((n − A)/D)` for `A ≤ n < A + D`, falling from 1;
    - `S` after that.
  - Let go: for `n ≥ G`, with `k = n − G` and `L = h(G)`, `L·(1 − k/R)` for `k < R`, and 0
    after. The release starts from wherever the envelope was, so letting go during the attack
    or decay does not jump.
  - The output is `h(n)` for `n < G`, then the release.
  - `A = 0` starts at 1, `D = 0` drops straight to `S`, and `R = 0` drops to 0 at `G`.
  - Both languages evaluate the expressions as written, so they agree to the bit.
- **The rules.**
  - `num_samples` at least 1.
  - `attack`, `decay`, `release` and `gate` at least 0. A gate past the end means the key is
    never let go.
  - `sustain` in [0, 1].

## Consequences

- **Vectors.**
  - Expected values come from the segments in exact rationals, rounded once.
  - The tolerance is `abs 1e-15, rel 1e-14`, for the two or three roundings of the float
    expressions.
