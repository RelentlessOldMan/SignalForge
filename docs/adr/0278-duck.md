# ADR 0278: Sidechain compressor

Status: accepted, 2026-11-17.

## Context

A sidechain compressor ducks one signal by the level of another, the key. The everyday case is
music that drops under a voice-over. The detector and gain law are those of the feed-forward
compressor (ADR 0257); only the detector's input changes.

## Decision

- **The API.**
  - Python: `duck(samples, key, threshold_db, ratio, attack, release, *, knee_db=0,
    makeup_db=0)` in `dsp`.
  - C#: `Dsp.Duck(samples, key, thresholdDb, ratio, attack, release, kneeDb = 0, makeupDb = 0)`.
  - `samples` real or complex, the output the same kind; `key` real.
- **The value.**
  - `e = envelope_follower(key, attack, release)` (ADR 0238).
  - `g[n] = compressor_gain(20·log10(e[n]), threshold_db, ratio, knee_db)` (ADR 0254) where
    `e[n] > 0`, and 0 where `e[n] = 0`.
  - `y[n] = samples[n]·10^((g[n] + makeup_db)/20)`; complex part by part.
  - `duck(x, x, …)` is `compress(x, …)` for real `x`.
- **The rules.**
  - The compressor's rules, with the detector's applied to `key`.
  - `key` must be real; `samples` must be finite and as long as `key`.

## Consequences

- **Vectors.**
  - The expected values reuse the compressor generator's transcription: the envelope in exact
    rationals, then stdlib floats for the level, the gain and the factor.
  - Instant cases are checked against the closed form.
  - The tolerance is the compressor's, `abs 1e-15, rel 1e-12`.
- **A complex key** would track `|key|`; it is left out until something needs it.
