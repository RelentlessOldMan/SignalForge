# ADR 0368: Standard normal homogeneity test

Status: accepted, 2026-11-18.

## Context

Alexandersson (1986) gave the standard normal homogeneity test (SNHT) for one shift in the
mean. It is the likelihood-ratio statistic for a single break in a standardized normal
record. With Pettitt (ADR 0366) and Buishand (ADR 0367), it makes up the usual set of
homogeneity tests for climate records.

## Decision

- **The API.**
  - Python: `snht(samples)` in `measurements`, giving a frozen dataclass
    `Snht(statistic, index)`.
  - C#: `Measurements.Snht(ReadOnlySpan<double> samples)`, giving the record
    `Snht(double Statistic, int Index)`.
- **The value.**
  - The samples are standardized, `z_i = (x_i − x̄)/σ`, by the float mean `(Σ x)/N` and the
    population standard deviation. Alexandersson's paper does not fix whether `σ` divides by
    `N` or `N − 1`, and implementations differ. SignalForge uses `N`.
  - For a split after `k` samples, `k = 1 … N − 1`, `T_k = k z̄₁² + (N − k) z̄₂²`, where `z̄₁`
    and `z̄₂` are the means of the two parts.
  - The `z` sum to 0, so `T_k = N S_k²/(k(N − k)σ²)`, where `S_k` is the sum of the first `k`
    deviations `x_i − x̄`, summed in order. Both languages compute it in that form, with the
    operations in that order.
  - `statistic = max_k T_k`.
  - `index` is `k − 1` for the smallest `k` reaching it. It is the last sample before the
    change, as in ADRs 0366 and 0367.
  - SignalForge does not give critical values. They come from simulation tables.
- **The rules.**
  - `samples` real, one-dimensional and finite.
  - `N ≥ 2`, and not every sample equal.

## Consequences

- **Vectors.**
  - The vector samples are dyadic, with an exact float mean (asserted).
  - Every `T_k` is then rational, so the generator takes each one exactly and finds the
    maximum and its location.
  - The tolerance is `abs 1e-13, rel 1e-13`.
