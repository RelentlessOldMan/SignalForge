# ADR 0276: Haas widener

Status: accepted, 2026-11-17.

## Context

When the same sound reaches the two ears a few milliseconds apart, the listener hears it from the
side that leads, not as an echo (the precedence or Haas effect). Delaying one channel of a mono
signal by about 1 to 30 ms widens it without changing its level on either side.

## Decision

- **The API.**
  - Python: `haas(samples, delay, *, side="right")` in `dsp`, returning a `LeftRight`
    (ADR 0264).
  - C#: `Dsp.Haas(samples, delay, side = StereoSide.Right)`, returning a `LeftRight`, with a new
    enum `StereoSide { Left, Right }`.
  - Real mono input only.
- **The value.**
  - The delayed side is `x[n − delay]`, 0 before time 0.
  - The other side is `x` unchanged.
  - Both sides have the input's length; samples past the end are dropped.
- **The rules.**
  - `delay` must be an integer `≥ 0`; 0 gives two copies of the input.
  - `side` must be `"left"` or `"right"` (C#: a defined `StereoSide`).
  - The samples must be real, finite and at least one.
- **Integer delays only.** A fractional delay is the `fractional_delay` of ADR 0040, applied to
  one channel.

## Consequences

- **Vectors.** Every case is exact, because a whole-sample shift does no arithmetic.
