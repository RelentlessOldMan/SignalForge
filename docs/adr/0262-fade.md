# ADR 0262: Fades

Status: accepted, 2026-11-17.

## Context

A signal that starts or stops abruptly clicks, and its spectrum spreads. A fade ramps the gain up
over the first samples and down over the last. The ramp is usually linear or a raised cosine,
which has zero slope at both ends. The exact first and last gains have to be fixed so that both
languages agree.

## Decision

- **The API.**
  - Python: `fade(samples, fade_in, fade_out, *, shape="linear")` in `dsp`.
  - C#: `Dsp.Fade(samples, fadeIn, fadeOut, shape = FadeShape.Linear)`, with the new enum
    `FadeShape { Linear, Cosine }`.
  - Python takes `"linear"` or `"cosine"`.
  - Real or complex in, the same kind out.
- **The ramp.** For `t` in `[0, 1)`:
  - linear: `r(t) = t`;
  - cosine: `r(t) = (1 − cos(π·t))/2`.
- **The gain.** With `N` samples and ramp lengths `Lᵢ` (in) and `Lₒ` (out), the gain starts at 1.
  - For `n < Lᵢ`, it is multiplied by `r(n/Lᵢ)`.
  - For `k = N − 1 − n < Lₒ`, it is multiplied by `r(k/Lₒ)`.
  - The first sample of a fade-in and the last of a fade-out are 0. The ramp reaches
    `r((L−1)/L)` at its inner end, and the next sample has gain 1.
  - The ramps may overlap, and then the gains multiply.
  - `y = x·g`. A complex sample is scaled part by part.
- **The rules.**
  - `fade_in` and `fade_out` must be integers `≥ 0`, each at most `N`.
  - `shape` must be one of the two names.
  - The samples must be finite and non-empty.

## Consequences

- **Vectors.**
  - Linear gains come from exact rationals.
  - Cosine gains come from the stdlib `math.cos`, checked by hand at `t = 0, 1/4, 1/2, 3/4`.
  - The tolerance is `abs 1e-15`.
- **Reuse.** `FadeShape` is the shape for a later crossfade.
