# 0019: Adjacent channel power ratio

Date: 2026-11-13. Status: accepted.

## Context

ACPR (also called ACLR) says how much of a transmitter's power lands in the channels next to its
own. Standards define the channels for their own signals; a framework needs one general rule that
any of those can be expressed in. Choices: what the channels are, how partial bins count, what the
ratio is relative to, and what happens at the edges of the spectrum.

## Decision

- **Built on channel power.** `acpr(frequencies, psd, center, bandwidth, offsets,
  adjacent_bandwidth=None, span=None)` integrates the PSD over the main channel and over a lower
  and an upper channel at each offset, with `channel_power`'s partial-bin rule, so ACPR and
  channel power always agree.
- **Offsets are center to center**, positive, any number of them, reported in the order given; the
  adjacent channels share one bandwidth, by default the main channel's. Standards with different
  bandwidths per offset call it once per bandwidth.
- **Ratios in dBc**, adjacent over main: negative for a clean transmitter, `−∞` for an empty
  channel. Lower and upper are reported separately; their worst or mean is the caller's choice.
- **Channels must lie inside the spectrum.** A channel that runs past the PSD's last bin would
  silently undercount. It is an error, so a wrong sample rate or offset shows at once.
- The PSD is an input, so any estimate (Welch with any window, a measured trace) can be used.
- Implemented from the definitions; no third-party code.

## Consequences

- Results depend on the PSD's resolution and window; the caller states those when quoting ACPR.
- No filtering of the channels (the root-raised-cosine weighting some standards use) yet; that
  would be an optional weight on the integral, later.
