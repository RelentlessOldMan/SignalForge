# ADR 0249: A-law companding

Status: accepted, 2026-11-17.

## Context

A-law is the European telephony counterpart of μ-law (ADR 0248). Its curve is linear near 0 and
logarithmic above `1/A`. As in ADR 0248, this is the continuous curve, not G.711's 8-bit segments.

## Decision

- **The API.**
  - Python: `a_law_compress(samples, a=87.6)` and `a_law_expand(samples, a=87.6)` in `dsp`.
  - C#: `Dsp.ALawCompress` and `Dsp.ALawExpand`, with the same defaults.
  - Real samples on `[−1, 1]`.
- **The value.** Let `L = 1 + ln A`.
  - Compress: `|y| = A|x|/L` if `|x| < 1/A`, else `(1 + ln(A|x|))/L`.
  - Expand: `|x| = |y|·L/A` if `|y| < 1/L`, else `exp(|y|·L − 1)/A`.
  - The sign is applied last, and `sign(0) = 0`.
  - Both branches meet at `|x| = 1/A`, `|y| = 1/L`, and that point goes to the log branch.
  - `A = 1` gives the identity: the whole range is the linear segment, and `L = 1`.
- **The rules.**
  - `a` must be finite and `≥ 1`.
  - The samples must be real, finite, non-empty and within `[−1, 1]`.

## Consequences

- **Vectors.**
  - Exact anchors: `±1` maps to `±1` and `0` to `0`. With `A = 1` the samples come back
    unchanged.
  - Other values come from the formula in the generator, using the standard library's `log`
    and `exp`, with a tolerance of about 1e-15. They are checked as a round trip and for
    continuity at the knee.
