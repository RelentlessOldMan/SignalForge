# ADR 0320: Bandlimited impulse train

Status: accepted, 2026-11-18.

## Context

A bandlimited impulse train (BLIT; Stilson and Smith, 1996) holds every harmonic of `f` below
Nyquist at equal level and nothing above. A naive train of single-sample impulses aliases. The
BLIT is the source that the classic alias-free sawtooth and square integrate, and is useful on
its own as a buzzy excitation for a vocal or resonator filter, and as a test comb with exactly
known lines.

## Decision

- **The API.**
  - Python: `blit(sample_rate, num_samples, frequency, amplitude=1, phase=0)` in `waveforms`.
  - C#: `Waveforms.Blit`, same arguments.
  - Real output, `phase` in cycles.
- **The value.**
  - `u[n]` is the oscillators' phase.
  - `H` is the number of harmonics `m ≥ 1` with `m·|f| < fs/2`: `H = ⌊fs/(2·|f|)⌋`, less 1
    when `H·|f| ≥ fs/2` in floating point. `M = 2·H + 1`.
  - The value is `y = A·(1 + 2·Σ_{m=1}^{H} cos(2π·m·u))/M`. The peak is `A` at `u = 0`, the
    mean is `A/M`, and every harmonic `m ≤ H` has amplitude `2·A/M`.
  - It is computed by the Dirichlet closed form. Take `v = u` for `u ≤ 1/2`, else `v = u − 1`,
    so `v` is small near both ends of the cycle. Then `y = A` when `v = 0`, else
    `y = A·(sin(M·(π·v))/(M·sin(π·v)))`.
  - The sign of `f` does not matter: the sum is even in `u`.
- **The rules.**
  - `sample_rate` positive and finite. `num_samples` an integer of at least 0.
  - `frequency` finite and non-zero with `|f| ≤ fs/2`. At `|f| = fs/2`, `H = 0` and `y = A`.
  - `amplitude` and `phase` finite.

## Consequences

- **Vectors.**
  - `H` comes from exact rationals, so the cases avoid `fs/(2·|f|)` that is close to an
    integer without being one.
  - The generator sums the cosines directly with `math.cos` and `math.fsum`, from `u` in exact
    rationals rounded once. That is the definition, not the closed form the code uses.
  - The tolerance is `abs 1e-12, rel 1e-12`.
