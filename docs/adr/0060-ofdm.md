# ADR 0060: OFDM modulate and demodulate

Status: accepted, 2026-11-13

## Context

OFDM carries symbols on orthogonal subcarriers: an inverse DFT per block, with a cyclic prefix so
a channel shorter than the prefix acts as one complex gain per subcarrier. Every OFDM standard
builds on this core, and each adds its own subcarrier plan, pilots, preambles and windowing. The
core has three choices two implementations could make differently: the DFT scaling, how
subcarriers are numbered and filled, and where the prefix comes from.

## Decision

- `ofdm_modulate(symbols, nfft, cp_len, subcarriers=None)` and
  `ofdm_demodulate(samples, nfft, cp_len, subcarriers=None)` in `signalforge.modulation`; C#
  `Modulation.OfdmModulate` and `Modulation.OfdmDemodulate`.
- **Unitary DFT scaling** (`1/√N` both ways). The time samples have the same energy as the
  symbols, so a block's mean sample power is the mean symbol energy times `U/N` for `U` used
  subcarriers, and demodulating a modulated block returns the symbols with no factor. Standards
  that scale differently scale the result.
- **Signed subcarrier indices** from `−⌊N/2⌋` to `N − 1 − ⌊N/2⌋`, as the shifted frequency axis
  numbers them: index `k` is FFT bin `k mod N`, at `k·fs/N`. The caller lists the used ones; the
  symbols fill them in the order listed, block after block. Unlisted subcarriers carry 0 (DC,
  guard bands). The default is every subcarrier, ascending.
- **Cyclic prefix** of `cp_len` samples, `0 ≤ cp_len ≤ N`: the block's last `cp_len` samples go
  in front of it. The demodulator drops them and takes the DFT of the rest; it does no timing,
  equalization or pilot handling.
- The symbol count must be a whole number of blocks, and the sample count a whole number of
  `N + cp_len`; a partial block is an error, not padding.

## Consequences

- A link or a standard waveform can be built from these with its own subcarrier map, and a
  multipath channel within the prefix can be checked as one gain per subcarrier.
- Pilots, preambles, windowing between blocks, synchronization and equalization are not here
  yet; they sit on top without changing these functions.
